FlyRank AI Internship · Week 01 · Draw the Path

Four pages, one door,
nothing else

The sitemap for a portfolio that has exactly one job: take a marketing lead who has never heard of me from cold landing to a requested audit. Every page below survives one test — does it move her along that line? Seven pages failed it and are listed at the bottom.

The claim
I make brands visible inside AI answers.
The one person
Marketing lead, B2B SaaS, 10–50 people, organic traffic flattening.
The one action
Request a free AI-visibility audit.

The map

Landing

  • Proof strip — three numbers, above the fold

  • CTA at the top and again at the bottom
Without it she never learns what I do.

STAGE 03
Action

  • No call required, no list, no follow-up sequence
The only place the belief is allowed to go.

Pages I am deliberately not building

Each of these exists on almost every portfolio I looked at. None of them move this person toward this action, so none of them ship.

RejectedWhy it does not earn its place
/blogA treadmill competing with the case studies for the same attention. If I have something to say, it becomes a case study.

/testimonialsA testimonial belongs inside the case study it came from, beside the numbers it vouches for.

What the pressure test changed

Version 1 of this map had six URLs and two ways to contact me. The tutor prompt — walk the path as her, cold, on a phone — broke three things.

Cut the /work index

Claim to proof went from two clicks to one. With three cases, an index page is pure friction.

Case studies open with the prompts

Verbatim, so she can paste them into ChatGPT herself. Proof she can check beats proof she has to trust.

/audit says what happens next

Deliverable, format, turnaround, and a no-call promise — the objection that stops a stranger handing over a company URL.
