# Proof statement

> One paragraph. One claim, one person, one action. Everything in the build gets measured
> against this paragraph — if a page doesn't serve it, the page doesn't ship.

---

I help B2B SaaS companies get **named inside AI answers**. My one person is the marketing lead at a
10–50 person B2B SaaS who is watching organic traffic flatten while ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's
AI Overviews answer their buyers' questions without ever citing them — she knows something changed,
she can't yet name it, and nobody on her team owns it. My claim is deliberately narrow: give me a
site and I will show which prompts its buyers actually ask, exactly where the brand is invisible
today, and what to change so it gets cited. The one action I want from her is to **request a free
AI-visibility audit of her site**.

**Why this matters beyond a CV or LinkedIn:** a CV says I've heard of this. A one-page report with
her own brand missing from twelve real buyer prompts — and her competitor named in nine of them —
says I can already do it. That gap between claiming and showing is the only thing that has ever
gotten me taken seriously, and a portfolio is the only place I control where it can exist.
